# The payer carries the Unclaimed residual

Money that nobody claims has still been spent, and the person who spent it is the payer — so when $46 of a dinner goes unclaimed, the payer is $46 out of pocket whatever the app decides. We charge that residual to the payer, **in the balance derivation rather than in the allocator**, so `Σ allocations + Unclaimed = Expense total` (ADR-0002) survives untouched and Unclaimed stays a named, displayable quantity. The objection this normally attracts — that it silently taxes the payer for the group's laziness — is real, and we answer it by removing the silence rather than by moving other people's money: the payer is told they are carrying it, and the whole group is shown that the bill is incomplete.

## Considered Options

- **Split the residual among the claimers, evenly or pro-rata.** The fair-sounding option, and the [balance prototype](https://github.com/fkeenv/tabby/issues/5) is the reason it loses: on a real scenario it charges Eve toward a dinner she did not attend. It makes claiming pointless in the exact case claiming exists for — if not claiming the fries costs you a share of the fries anyway, the prompt is decoration. Recorded here because it sounds reasonable enough to be proposed again.
- **Leave it unallocated: nobody absorbs it, and the books simply do not balance.** The only fully honest option and the only unusable one. Under it the nets sum to the residual instead of zero, so **Settled becomes permanently unreachable** — one unclaimed fry would switch off a first-class derived state forever — and the prototype rendered it as a red imbalance banner that no amount of design could make acceptable on a screen whose job is to show one number.
- **Absorbing the residual inside the allocator, as a real Allocation to the payer.** Rejected on modelling grounds rather than arithmetic: it drives Unclaimed to zero by construction, which deletes the concept, breaks the invariant in ADR-0002, and smears the policy across the allocator and its golden vectors. Keeping it one layer up means reversing this decision is a change to one function.
- **An explicit Organizer action to write off or reassign a residual.** Unnecessary once the payer carries it, because the residual no longer blocks anything. The close-out already exists: since the Claim Link has no identity boundary (ADR-0003), *"nobody is claiming the fries"* is resolved by claiming the fries, and when the payer does it for themselves not a penny moves — they were already carrying it — while the residual and its nudge go to zero.

## Consequences

- **The payer's Balance no longer derives from their Claims and Payments alone.** It also charges them the Unclaimed residual of every Expense they paid for. `CONTEXT.md` says so; anything computing a Balance must.
- **Unclaimed becomes visible on the Participant balance screen**, narrowly, amending the prototype's ruling that it never appears there. The payer sees it named inside the existing *"Why them?"* disclosure, because they are the one being charged. Everyone sees a soft group-level nudge — *the bill is not fully claimed, was any of it yours?* — linking into the claim screen, because the people who ate the fries are looking at this screen and the Organizer's ledger is not where a residual gets resolved.
- **The nudge is derived, not dismissible.** It shows while the residual is non-zero and the Group is not Settled, and disappears on its own. No flag, no new state, and no UI suggestion to delete the Line Item — the money was spent and the Expense total should keep saying so.
- **Pro-rata Adjustments gain a synthetic Unclaimed weight**, so the tax and tip attributable to an unclaimed Line Item follow it to the payer instead of quietly landing on the claimers. Refines ADR-0002's weighting; the allocator's golden vectors need a case for it.
- **Every Expense's residual follows its own payer.** A Group with three payers has three residuals going three different ways, which falls out for free and needs no aggregation anywhere except the group-level nudge.
