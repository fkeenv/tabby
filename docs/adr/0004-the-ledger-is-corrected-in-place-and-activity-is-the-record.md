# The ledger is corrected in place and Activity is the record

An Organizer will mistype a price, forget an item, and record the same dinner twice, and every one of those corrections lands on Claims that already exist and silently moves money that people may already have paid. We chose to let corrections land **in place** — Claims survive the edit beneath them, Balances re-derive, and nothing is versioned — and to protect the group with **visibility instead**: an append-only **Activity** stream of every change, readable by anyone holding the Claim Link. This follows ADR-0003: where nothing can be verified, being able to see what happened is the only defence there is, and it has to be available to the people it protects, none of whom have accounts.

## Considered Options

- **Per-record revision history: old and new values on every Expense edit.** The thorough answer, and rejected as the wrong shape for the problem. What a group needs is *"the pizza went up, and it was Keen"*, which a single readable line delivers; what revision history additionally buys is reconstructing the bill as it stood on Tuesday, which nobody has asked for and which costs a versioned schema on every ledger table. Recorded here because it is genuinely hard to reverse — the data to reconstruct history is never captured, so adopting it later starts from the day it ships, not from the beginning.
- **Invalidating Claims when the Line Item beneath them changes materially.** Superficially safer: make people re-confirm what they ate. Rejected because it misreads what a Claim asserts. A Claim says *I consumed some of this*, and a corrected price does not falsify that — it only changes what the assertion costs. Under this option a typo fix would silently discard the work of five people who are no longer at the table to redo it.
- **Refusing to delete anything that has Claims on it, requiring the Organizer to zero it out.** Rejected on the leading use case: a duplicated Expense is precisely the thing that most needs deleting, and it will have Claims on it by the time anyone notices.
- **An Organizer-only audit log.** Rejected as protecting the one person who does not need protecting. The Organizer is the only actor whose authority is real; the log exists for everybody else.

## Consequences

- **Deletion is soft throughout the ledger.** An Expense or Line Item carries `deleted_at`, its Claims are preserved and excluded from Balances, and the Activity entry describing the deletion outlives the row it describes — an append-only stream that loses entries when the underlying record goes is not append-only. Activity is purged only with the Group, at the same 30-day hard purge, so the product keeps one retention number rather than two.
- **Warn, never block.** Every correction is legitimate, so nothing is forbidden: the delete confirmation names how many people claimed it, the edit form shows the claimer count, and changing an Expense's payer warns when the outgoing payer has already received a Payment and may be holding money they are not owed. The app names the consequence; the humans settle it.
- **Quantity gains no new meaning.** An Organizer dropping a quantity below the Shares already claimed produces exactly the over-claimed state a table of people can produce by hand, and reads the same amber. Quantity is a completeness warning, not a cap, and arriving at the state from the ledger side does not make it an error.
- **Claims are recorded in Activity, rolled up.** Because anyone may claim as anyone, *"someone claimed three Shares as Alice"* is the single most useful line in the stream — but one entry per tap is unreadable, so Claims collapse per Participant per Expense while ledger mutations and Payments record individually.
- **A zero crossing is announced, not merely logged.** ADR-0001 makes a square Participant returning to debt intended behaviour; it is also the only change that contradicts something the app previously told that person, so the Participant balance screen says so outright rather than letting the number move quietly.
- **"Since you last looked" is device-scoped, not person-scoped.** The marker lives beside the Acting Participant in the `tabby_claim` cookie, because ADR-0003 leaves nobody to attribute a read to — and on the shared phone the design assumes, per-Participant read state would be corrupted by whoever picked it up second.
